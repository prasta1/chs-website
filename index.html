Studying and preserving the history of our small part of the world.

The Cambridge Historical Society, located in Cambridge Vermont, is dedicated to studying and preserving the history of our small part of the world. Located just over the mountain from Stowe, Cambridge is proud of its commitment to its heritage.

We meet every 2nd Wednesday of the month at the Warner Lodge, 49 School St., Jeffersonville, VT. Programs are free and open to the public.

30 years of the Cambridge Greenway Path

7:00 p.m. · Warner Lodge, 49 School Street · Free · Handicap accessible

We’ll gather at the Cambridge Historical Society to hear the story of this beloved 1.25-mile recreational corridor along the Lamoille River. Laird MacDowell and Zeke Zucker will share how the Greenway was created on privately owned land through legally established easements, and how ongoing stewardship has helped protect it from river-related flood damage through strategic riverbank armoring.

They’ll also highlight the Greenway’s important role today as a connector for Lamoille Valley Rail Trail users, providing easy access to Village of Jeffersonville amenities and supporting the local economy. All are welcome. Refreshments will be served.

Elsewhere in the valley
Vermont Covered Bridge Society
Plaque for the VCBS 25th anniversary

A plaque recognizing the 25th anniversary of the Vermont Covered Bridge Society was dedicated at the Cambridge Junction Bridge near Jeffersonville. The organization was founded to preserve Vermont’s covered bridges, and its first project was to rescue the historic Cambridge Junction bridge that was in danger of collapsing into the Lamoille River.

The 140-foot Burr arch structure was originally built by the town of Cambridge in 1887 for $3,520; the repairs cost almost a million dollars. It reopened to traffic in 2004. vermontbridges.org
